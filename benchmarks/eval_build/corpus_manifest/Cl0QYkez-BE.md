## L2 summary
The 2011 Nobel Memorial Prize Lectures by economists Thomas Sargent and Christopher Sims explore empirical and theoretical frameworks for understanding cause and effect in macroeconomics, focusing on the interplay between public expectations and economic policy. Sargent applies structural econometrics and rational expectations theory to historical U.S. fiscal case studies from 1780 to 1840, illustrating how institutional frameworks, default probabilities, and budget constraints shape sovereign debt values and offer insights for modern debt crises. In parallel, Sims traces the evolution of econometric modeling from early Keynesian systems to modern Bayesian inference and structural vector autoregressions (SVARs), demonstrating how policy choices can be systematically evaluated without relying on arbitrary statistical assumptions. Together, their contributions provide foundational tools for analyzing monetary adjustments, fiscal governance, and macroeconomic stability.
