## L2 summary
This document summarizes Richard Thaler's 2017 Nobel Prize lecture, detailing the evolution and application of behavioral economics. It explores how psychological factors—such as loss aversion, mental accounting, status quo bias, and "supposedly irrelevant factors" (SIFs)—cause real human behavior to deviate systematically from the fully rational models of traditional economics. The summary highlights key empirical experiments, such as the Cornell mug study, and demonstrates how choice architecture and libertarian paternalism led to practical real-world solutions like automatic pension enrollment and the "Save More Tomorrow" program. Finally, using long-term data from sources like the Swedish pension system, the text emphasizes how behavioral nudges remain highly effective over time to improve public policy and financial decision-making.
