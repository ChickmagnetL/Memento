## L3 brief
David Card reviews the rise of design-based empirical methods and natural experiments in labor economics, demonstrating how transparent research designs challenge conventional economic models and provide reliable causal insights.

## L2 summary
In his Nobel Prize lecture, David Card discusses the evolution and impact of design-based empirical approaches in labor economics. Addressing the historical credibility gap caused by unreliable macro models and flawed econometric methods, Card highlights how natural and quasi-experiments—such as his studies on the Mariel boatlift and minimum wage increases—provided clear, causal insights that challenged conventional economic assumptions. He outlines the core strengths of research design, including transparent causality, direct policy relevance, and portability across different contexts, while addressing common criticisms regarding specificity and model complexity. Ultimately, Card argues that robust research designs have pushed mainstream economics to accept alternative market frameworks, such as employer wage-setting power, and encourages young researchers to continue pursuing transparent empirical methodologies.
