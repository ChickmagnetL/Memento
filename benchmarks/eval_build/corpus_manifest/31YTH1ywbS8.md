## L3 brief
Douglas Diamond explains the mechanisms of financial intermediation, detailing how banks perform delegated monitoring, create liquidity through short-term debt, and become vulnerable to bank runs.

## L2 summary
In his Nobel Prize lecture, Douglas Diamond outlines his foundational research on financial intermediation, banking mechanisms, and system stability. He details how banks act as delegated monitors to mitigate conflicts of interest through asset diversification (pooling and tranching), while also explaining how banks create liquidity by issuing short-term debt backed by long-term illiquid assets. Furthermore, Diamond discusses how this structural reliance on short-term funding inherent to financial institutions leaves the system inherently susceptible to self-fulfilling bank runs and panics, emphasizing the role of government interventions like deposit insurance in mitigating systemic risks.
