## L3 brief
This introductory lecture outlines the scope and goals of MIT's 14.02 Principles of Macroeconomics course, using recent global economic events to demonstrate how central bank policies, inflation, and market dynamics shape macroeconomics.

## L2 summary
In this introductory lecture for MIT's 14.02 Principles of Macroeconomics, the instructor outlines the course structure and core concepts, emphasizing that macroeconomics analyzes aggregate economy-wide phenomena—such as inflation, unemployment, interest rates, and global supply chains—rather than individual households or firms. Unlike high-level academic research, the course focuses on conceptual, non-math-heavy models designed to help students critically digest real-world economic publications like the *Wall Street Journal* or the IMF's *World Economic Outlook*. Through examples including post-COVID-19 inflation spikes, central bank interest rate policies, equity market reactions, and China's economic reopening, the instructor demonstrates how macroeconomic factors interact and sets expectations for using simplified models throughout the semester.
