## L3 brief
Abhijit Banerjee demonstrates how field experiments and randomized controlled trials have transformed economics by providing nuanced empirical evidence to address major questions surrounding global poverty and human behavior.

## L2 summary
In his 2019 Nobel Prize lecture, Abhijit Banerjee addresses common criticisms of using randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and field experiments in economics. He refutes the claims that RCTs lack generalizability, tackle only minor issues, and produce piecemeal data by demonstrating how experimental research uncovers non-standard human behaviors (such as the key role of "gossips" in spreading social information) and offers compelling evidence for major economic phenomena like poverty traps. Ultimately, Banerjee contends that RCTs have fundamentally reshaped economic practice by enabling researchers to systematically test behavioral hypotheses, liberate economic thought from rigid theoretical premises, and build robust, evidence-backed frameworks to help alleviate global poverty.
