## L3 brief
Claudia Goldin's 2023 Nobel lecture details the historical evolution of women's labor force participation and demonstrates how "greedy occupations" requiring long, inflexible hours continue to drive the modern gender pay gap.

## L2 summary
In her 2023 Nobel Prize lecture, economic historian and labor economist Claudia Goldin outlines the historical evolution and persistent challenges regarding women's participation in the labor market. She explains how female employment historically followed a U-shaped pattern, initially declining as work shifted away from the home before rising significantly alongside increased educational attainment, reduced social stigma, and institutional milestones like access to the birth control pill. This evolution culminated in a "quiet revolution" where young women shifted from merely seeking paid jobs to pursuing long-term careers and higher education. However, Goldin notes that despite narrowing gaps in education and experience, a persistent wage gap remains—largely driven by "greedy occupations" that disproportionately reward long, inflexible hours. Because couples with caregiving responsibilities often divide labor so that one parent takes the higher-paying, less flexible job, gender inequality persists, highlighting the need for workplace structures that make flexibility less costly.
