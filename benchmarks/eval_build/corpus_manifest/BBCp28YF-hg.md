## L3 brief
Ben Bernanke illustrates how banking and credit market stress amplifies economic downturns during historical financial crises and routine business cycles, emphasizing the necessity of robust financial regulation.

## L2 summary
In his 2022 Nobel Prize lecture, Ben Bernanke explains how stress in banking and credit markets elevates the external finance premium, severely restricting credit availability and causing broad macroeconomic downturns. Drawing on case studies of the Great Depression and the 2007–2009 global financial crisis, he demonstrates that banking panics and borrower insolvencies exacerbate recessions far more than traditional models once predicted. Furthermore, Bernanke shows how these credit market dynamics amplify normal business cycles through the financial accelerator and monetary policy channels, underscoring the critical need for strong regulatory oversight and effective crisis intervention tools.
