## L3 brief
Economist Daron Acemoglu delivers his 2024 Nobel lecture explaining how inclusive versus extractive institutions shape national prosperity, institutional persistence, and the economic impacts of emerging technologies like AI.

## L2 summary
In his 2024 Nobel Prize lecture in Economic Sciences, Daron Acemoglu explores how societal institutions fundamentally drive global economic disparities. He outlines how European colonial history demonstrates a causal relationship between institutions and prosperity, where inclusive institutions fostered long-term wealth while extractive institutions concentrated power among elites and locked nations into persistent poverty. Introducing the Utility Technology Possibilities Frontier framework, Acemoglu illustrates the political mechanisms—such as the fear of becoming economic or political losers—that cause elites to resist beneficial institutional and technological change. Ultimately, he applies these historical insights to modern technological shifts, emphasizing that choices surrounding artificial intelligence can either empower workers and promote shared prosperity or exacerbate inequality through excessive automation.
