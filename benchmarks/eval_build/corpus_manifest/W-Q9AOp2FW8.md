## L3 brief
This document examines how financial deregulation, complex derivatives like credit default swaps, and predatory subprime mortgage practices culminated in the catastrophic 2008 global financial crisis and its widespread impact on Main Street.

## L2 summary
This document details the origins, systemic expansion, and catastrophic fallout of the 2008 global financial crisis as featured in FRONTLINE's "Money, Power and Wall Street." Beginning with JPMorgan's creation of credit default swaps to manage risk, the financial sector rapidly transformed these instruments into complex synthetic CDOs and bundled subprime mortgage products. Despite warnings and regulatory efforts from figures like Brooksley Born, resistance from deregulatory leaders and aggressive banking lobby efforts left the multi-trillion-dollar derivatives market largely unmonitored. When the U.S. housing bubble collapsed, the interconnected webs of toxic assets triggered widespread bank failures, massive taxpayer bailouts, and severe, lasting devastation for local communities across Main Street.
